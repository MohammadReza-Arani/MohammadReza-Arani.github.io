Radar & RF Systems 5 GHz • Range-Doppler Map • MATLAB

Traffic Movement RF Sensor for Violation Detection

Have you ever considered any other option for tracking, managing, and controlling roads and streets rather than widely used Traffic Cameras?

In today's world with ever-increasing life speed and demanding transportation needs, managing the high volume of traffic in the streets smoothly and swiftly is vital. While considering the high data volume associated with cameras and the intensive processing time of such computer vision systems, it is difficult to handle real-time traffic data at low latency.

With modern chipsets handling high sampling rates, we can obtain precise data from an RF sensor working at 77 GHz and 5 GHz with commercial affordability. To obtain sufficient kinematic information from a moving vehicle, specific bandwidth and high resolution in both Range and Doppler frequency are necessary to estimate velocity and position accurately.

At the University of Tehran, we designed and simulated traffic volume estimation and kinematic extraction from vehicles in MATLAB. Below, the 5 GHz Range-Doppler (R-D) Map and corresponding XY plane localization are demonstrated:

Slotted Substrate Integrated Waveguide Sensor (77 GHz)

What is the incentive to engineer such a variety of Sensors in the market?

Physical constraints, volume limitations, and manufacturing expense guide sensor design. For autonomous vehicles' RF sensors, conventional waveguides are bulky and costly. Starting from leaky-wave principles, I designed a conformal annular slotted waveguide sensor.

To transform this bulky design into a low-profile, highly integrated, and cost-effective unit, I designed a Slotted Substrate Integrated Waveguide (SSIW) Annular Sensor at 77 GHz and simulated this unit in Ansys HFSS.

Optimization: Quadratic Cost-Function

One of the most powerful optimization methods when dealing with convex and non-convex problems is the Gradient Descent method family. Here, both Steepest Descent and Newton's Method are investigated for minimizing the quadratic cost function:

$$ f(x) = x_{1}^{2} + x_{2}^{2} - 4x_{1} - 6x_{2} + 13 + x_{1}x_{2} $$

Neurotechnology & ML EEG Signals • CSP Filter • LDA Classifier • 90% Accuracy

Brain-Computer Interface: EEG Action Classification via ML

In this project, we tackled motor-imagery electroencephalography (EEG) signal decoding to classify 4 distinct motor intentions:

  • Class 1: Movement of Right Finger
  • Class 2: Movement of Right Arm
  • Class 3: Movement of Right Leg
  • Class 4: Rest / No Action

We engineered a processing pipeline utilizing Common Spatial Pattern (CSP) spatial filters followed by a Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) classifier in the mapped spatial domain. Pre-whitened signal matrices allowed the classifier to achieve approximately 90% classification accuracy.

Finite Difference Time Domain: Wave Propagation

Numerical methods in electromagnetics form the core engine of industrial simulation suites such as Ansys HFSS, CST Studio, and COMSOL. Here, the Finite Difference Time Domain (FDTD) technique is employed to solve Maxwell's curl equations numerically on a discretized spatial-temporal grid.

Quantum Computing & AI QAOA • Adiabatic Quantum Computing • Hamiltonian • Python

Quantum Machine Learning & Parameterized Circuit Optimization

Quantum Machine Learning merges quantum mechanical principles with classical optimization paradigms to solve high-dimensional combinatorial and physics-based problems intractable for classical architectures.

The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) numerically differentiates parameterized quantum circuits to interface with classical optimizers (e.g. Adam) to tune gate angles. Under Adiabatic Quantum Computing (AQC), Hamiltonian minimization evolves the system ground state toward the optimal problem solution:

$$ Cost = \| F( ec{ heta}) - 0.4 \|^{2} $$

where $F( ec{ heta})$ denotes the expectation value of the quantum circuit and the objective is framed to minimize Mean Square Error (MSE):

Antenna Arrays & Beamforming Array Factor • Convex Sparsity • Side Lobe Level (SLL)

Optimization of 1-D Array to Reach Desired Pattern

Antenna array optimization synthesizes radiation patterns satisfying rigorous physical constraints and minimal element counts. We formulate 1-D array synthesis by minimizing the MSE cost function between the synthesized array factor $AF( heta)$ and a target envelope $g( heta)$:

$$ J = \| AF( heta) - g( heta) \|^{2} $$

By coupling convex optimization with $\ell_1$-norm regularization, we achieve a sparse antenna arrangement with minimized elements while maintaining the desired Side Lobe Level (SLL). Below is the comparison between uniform and optimized array configurations:
