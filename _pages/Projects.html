MIMO Radar Systems & Sensory Arrays Master's Graduate Thesis Advisors: Dr. S. Akhavan & Prof. K. PoorAghdam

Design & Optimization of 1-D & 2-D Sensory Arrays for Tracking Offending Vehicles (Master's Thesis)

Have you ever considered any other option for tracking, managing, and controlling roads and streets rather than widely used Traffic Cameras?

In today's world with ever-increasing life speed and demanding transportation needs, managing the high volume of traffic smoothly and swiftly is vital. While optical cameras are widely deployed, their high data volume, vulnerability to adverse weather or lighting conditions, and computationally intensive computer vision pipelines pose major bottlenecks for low-latency violation detection.

To address these challenges, my Master's thesis at the University of Tehran focused on the design and mathematical optimization of 1-D and 2-D sensory arrays capable of tracking offending and violating vehicles in roads using MIMO (Multiple-Input Multiple-Output) radar systems. By synthesizing tailored radiation patterns and suppressing sidelobe levels, MIMO radar achieves high angular resolution with a reduced number of physical RF transceivers.

Key scientific and algorithmic contributions include:

  • 1-D & 2-D Sensory Array Optimization: Formulated sensory array synthesis using both convex optimization and non-convex optimizers, alongside Gradient Descent (GD) algorithms to optimize element positions, phase weights, and excitation amplitudes for maximum directivity and sidelobe suppression.
  • Multi-Target Kinematic Tracking: Deployed Kalman Filtering to continuously track vehicles' kinematic state vectors (range, radial velocity, azimuth angle) across consecutive radar frames to identify speeding, lane violations, and erratic maneuvers.
  • Clutter & Spatial Noise Clustering: Integrated the DBSCAN (Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise) algorithm to cluster dense radar point clouds and eliminate multi-path reflections and environmental ground clutter before feeding detections to the kinematic tracking filters.

The entire MIMO radar pipeline, array synthesis, and kinematic estimators were modeled and simulated in MATLAB, evaluated across 5 GHz and 77 GHz automotive millimeter-wave radar bands:

Slotted Substrate Integrated Waveguide Sensor (77 GHz)

What is the incentive to engineer such a variety of Sensors in the market?

Physical constraints, volume limitations, and manufacturing expense guide sensor design. For autonomous vehicles' RF sensors, conventional waveguides are bulky and costly. Starting from leaky-wave principles, I designed a conformal annular slotted waveguide sensor.

To transform this bulky design into a low-profile, highly integrated, and cost-effective unit, I designed a Slotted Substrate Integrated Waveguide (SSIW) Annular Sensor at 77 GHz and simulated this unit in Ansys HFSS.

Neurotechnology & ML EEG Signals • CSP Filter • LDA Classifier • 90% Accuracy

Brain-Computer Interface: EEG Action Classification via ML

In this project, we tackled motor-imagery electroencephalography (EEG) signal decoding to classify 4 distinct motor intentions:

  • Class 1: Movement of Right Finger
  • Class 2: Movement of Right Arm
  • Class 3: Movement of Right Leg
  • Class 4: Rest / No Action

We engineered a processing pipeline utilizing Common Spatial Pattern (CSP) spatial filters followed by a Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) classifier in the mapped spatial domain. Pre-whitened signal matrices allowed the classifier to achieve approximately 90% classification accuracy.

Quantum Machine Learning & Parameterized Circuit Optimization

Quantum Machine Learning merges quantum mechanical principles with classical optimization paradigms to solve high-dimensional combinatorial and physics-based problems intractable for classical architectures.

The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) numerically differentiates parameterized quantum circuits to interface with classical optimizers (e.g. Adam) to tune gate angles. Under Adiabatic Quantum Computing (AQC), Hamiltonian minimization evolves the system ground state toward the optimal problem solution:

Parameterized Quantum Circuit Cost: $$ Cost = \| F( ec{ heta}) - 0.4 \|^{2} $$

Optimization of 1-D Array to Reach Desired Pattern

Antenna array optimization synthesizes radiation patterns satisfying rigorous physical constraints and minimal element counts. We formulate 1-D array synthesis by minimizing the MSE cost function between the synthesized array factor $AF(\theta)$ and a target envelope $g(\theta)$:

Synthesis Cost Function: $$ J = \| AF(\theta) - g(\theta) \|^{2} $$

Enterprise Security & Systems Architecture Active Research (2025–Present) ISO/IEC 27001 • NIST CSF • SABSA • TOGAF

DevSecOps Fundamentals & Enterprise Architecture Implementation

How can modern automated DevSecOps pipelines successfully permeate complex organizational hierarchies without stalling under bureaucratic routines?

While continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD) pipelines offer unparalleled deployment velocity, integrating robust security (Shift-Left) across enterprise-wide ecosystems often encounters organizational resistance, compliance friction, and rigid bureaucratic routines.

This active research project investigates the theoretical foundations and applied frameworks of DevSecOps, conducting an exhaustive literature review to establish a unified operational model tailored for enterprise adoption. The research develops actionable blueprints to harmonize automated security telemetry with formal governance, risk, and compliance (GRC) workflows.

The architectural synthesis bridges four internationally recognized standards:

  • ISO/IEC 27001: Mapping automated vulnerability assessments, artifact signing, and pipeline access controls to Annex A security control objectives and continuous audit trails.
  • NIST SP 800-53 & NIST CSF: Implementing risk mitigation matrices, continuous authorization (cATO), software supply chain security (NIST SSDF), and automated compliance enforcement.
  • SABSA (Sherwood Applied Business Security Architecture): Developing business-driven security matrices to guarantee end-to-end traceability from contextual enterprise goals down to component-level pipeline security policies.
  • TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework): Embedding DevSecOps capabilities into enterprise architecture domains (Business, Data, Application, and Technology) via the TOGAF Architecture Development Method (ADM).
